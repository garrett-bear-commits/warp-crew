# Warp Crew universe proposal (2026-10-09)

Status: **proposal for Garrett** ("I'm open to suggestions"). It builds on what the game already says: the 50 crew
bios, the sector blurbs and the story beats. Names marked *new* are proposals; everything else already exists in
`src/data/`.

## The pitch in three lines

The lanes between the stars are the only roads, and something is eating them. The navy went home, so the frontier
runs on contracts. You own a cheap ship with a bad history, and a crew nobody else would hire.

**Tone:** dangerous, funny, strange. Firefly's working-class crew, FTL's desperate fights, Cowboy Bebop's bounties,
a dash of Hitchhiker's absurdity. Never grimdark, never slapstick. Short sentences, concrete nouns, dry jokes
(the voice already in the game: "The clerks are polite, slow and armed. Your papers are mostly real.").

## The world

- **The Lanes.** Faster-than-light travel only works along warp lanes: ancient corridors between stars. The
  *Lane Compact* (*new*), an old human and alien commonwealth, mapped them, gated them and grew rich on them.
- **The Hush** (*new*). Fifty years ago the Compact's core worlds went quiet. No war, no message: they just
  stopped answering. The navy pulled back to guard what was left. The frontier arm, **the Spur**, was left to its
  haulers, clinics, scrapyards and pirates.
- **The Eclipse Swarm.** A bio-machine ecology that grows along the lanes and nests in jump wakes. It is drawn to
  jump noise, so every jump feeds it a little. Probes tag convoys, broods swallow stations, and lately the Swarm
  has started to *learn*: Eclipse, the Mythic merc, is a Swarm gun-form that chose a name.
- **The mystery** (seeded already: "a lane that looks back", Voidwake "the lane that learned to sit in a
  chair", the crypt that plays "a captain who sounds like you, from a war you have not fought yet"):
  - The lanes were not built. They were *grown*, by something that sleeps beyond the Null Meridian.
  - The Swarm is its immune system, waking up.
  - The Compact's core didn't fall: it was *swallowed into a lane*, and time runs strangely in there.
  - That is the long-running secret the main story and the seasons peel back, one reveal per arc.

| Hiring (gacha) | A hiring beacon broadcast down the lanes. Who answers is luck; the reveal is an incoming transmission and a docking pod. |

## Eight crews (factions)

The game tags each merc with one of 23 faction labels. Folding them into eight families gives synergy bonuses
something to stand on, and each family later gets contracts, a shop and a standing.

| Family | Tags folded in | Who they are | Fight identity (for synergies) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haulers' Union** | haulers, convoy, market | Space truckers and traders; the frontier's backbone | Salvage and pay bonuses, fuel thrift |
| **The Yards** | yard, forge | Scrapyard droids and shipwrights; the Forge Moon AI | Faster repairs and system recovery |
| **Free Wings** | privateers, cartel | Corsairs with a code (mostly); smugglers | Crits, weapon charge |
| **Remnant Navy** | navy, station | What's left of the Compact fleet; washouts and veterans | Shields, discipline, boarder defence |
| **Haven** | clinic, colony, haven | Clinics and colonies: the people you protect | Healing, crew survival |
| **The Survey** | survey | Mapmakers and signal-chasers | Evasion, revealing enemy rooms, away-team success |
| **The Choirs** | crystal, tidefall, cloud, rings | Alien lineages: crystal-lattice traders, bubble-skinned Tidefallers | Odd tricks: ion, shield harmonics |
| **The Unbound** | defector, void, prototype, silicate, hollow | Strange ones: Swarm defectors, rogue AIs, the lane-touched | Rule-breakers (most top-rarity mercs) |

## Recurring characters (*new*)

- **Auntie Vell:** four-armed Tidefall fixer who runs the Exchange. She gives most contracts and has an opinion
  about your hull. "Everyone pays. Some of you pay in installments."
- **Captain Silas Crane** of the *Gilded Hound*: a bounty hunter who keeps beating you to jobs. A rival, later a
  reluctant ally, possibly a boss in a season.
- **Commodore Ines Tarrow:** the last Compact officer in the Spur. She holds the gates and decides who passes. She
  explains why walls exist and wants you to prove you're worth the fuel.
- **The Choir:** the Swarm's voice. It speaks in your own hull ID read backwards ("A station sings your hull ID
  backward" is already a beat). The antagonist presence; it never shows a face.
- **The last captain of the Sparrow:** you bought the Sparrow cheap because her captain vanished mid-jump. Her
  black box holds a corrupted log, in your voice. This is the main story thread.

## The main story: "The Long Jump" (five acts across the five sectors)

1. **The Spur:** buy the Sparrow, answer the distress call, meet Auntie Vell. Swarm probes start tagging convoys.
   The black box plays its first broken line. Boss: the corsair flagship holding the Veil Gate.
2. **Veil Edge:** wraith ships that move when you look away; Haven's last open clinic. You follow the last
   captain's trail. Boss: the navy frigate at the Ember gate. Tarrow won't let just anyone through.
3. **Ember Reach:** the Forge Moon. A burned ledger shows someone pouring warships in secret ("Ash Ledger" already
   exists). Silas Crane is on the same trail.
4. **Hollow Expanse:** comms go polite and the dark is not empty. The Choir sings. The crypt plays your voice from
   a war you haven't fought yet.
5. **Crown Halo:** the Throne is a Compact flagship the Swarm swallowed. Its AI still issues orders from an empty
   chair. You win, the war doesn't end, and the lane beyond the unfinished gate looks back.

**After the story:** the Null Meridian becomes **the Rift**, the endless procedural frontier. Each season opens a
new region or storyline: Season 1 *The Ember Uprising*, Season 2 *What the Choir Wants*, Season 3 *Crane's
Bounty*, and so on.

## How the story is delivered (light by default, deep if you want it)

- One evocative line per contract card.
- Story moments as 2–3 panel transmissions with a portrait, skippable, never more than about 40 words.
- Crew barks inside the ship after big moments: a win, a near miss, a new recruit, a level-up.
- Bond scenes and loyalty missions per merc (where the 50 written bios finally appear).
- The Almanac for lore lovers: wanted posters, crew files, sector notes, the black-box logs.
