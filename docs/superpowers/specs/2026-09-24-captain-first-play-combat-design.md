# Captain-first onboarding and readable combat

Status: design approved by Garrett on 2026-09-24; not an implementation or release approval

Date: 2026-09-24

Supersedes the first-session order and guided-combat action in the [2026-09-22 living-ship design](2026-09-22-living-ship-core-vertical-slice-design.md). Its save, fairness, camera, and release-safety boundaries still apply.

## Player promise and package boundary

In the first two minutes, the player chooses and names a captain who actually works aboard the Sparrow, hires one crewmate, puts them at a visible station, and saves a trader from pirates by making one useful targeting decision. The crew visibly carries out the fight. A win pays out, opens the third berth, and leads to a random free crew pull. The player then returns to a usable ship with a clear next job. This should feel like commanding people on a ship, not advancing text cards.

This package replaces the fresh-save first session and the first repeatable pirate fight, plus the screens and art needed to make those actions legible. It does not rebalance the whole economy, change reputation odds, add purchases, overhaul every encounter, or claim physical-device/Jest readiness. PR #1 remains unmerged and Jest production inactive without Garrett's explicit approval. GitHub Pages, if separately published, remains mock-platform QA only.

## Decision and alternatives

Use a new tutorial script and four equal-tier starter captain templates. Each is a normal working crew instance with a protected captain marker and a player-set display name. The four visual identities are a cyborg-eyed human man (pilot), a warm-brown-skinned human woman (gunner), a distinctive alien (scout), and a small droid (engineer), tied to the approved five-crew splash. Each starts Common, one star, at equivalent baseline power. Their role changes the useful station or Away assignment, but not the chance of winning the first fight. The unchosen templates enter the ordinary Common recruit pool; a later duplicate of the chosen template upgrades the captain without replacing their custom name. The captain cannot be sold, benched, or removed by overflow, but can work, level, take injury, and join an Away team like other crew.

Considered alternatives: reusing Rex/Kira/Nemi/Tink directly would make captain choice grant unequal rarity and would pair some portraits with the wrong splash identity; keeping the existing Rex/Bolt tutorial and adding a captain-selection pop-up would leave the promised hire-and-staff sequence fictitious. The four-template approach adds catalog and migration work, but makes the choice real and keeps ordinary recruitment coherent.

The first hire is free and curated, not a gacha pull or a paid offer. Offer Jen Park (Common gunner) when the chosen captain is not a gunner; offer Bolt (Common engineer) when the captain is the gunner. The hire card shows a clear portrait, role, one concrete job benefit, and a single **Hire for free** action. This gives every starting pair a gunner and a second useful station without forcing a multi-option shop choice. The welcome pull comes only after the win, is the first recorded pull, and is guaranteed Uncommon using the existing Kira/Tink/Nemi pool and pity/history logic. It cannot silently turn into a duplicate of the starter captain because the starter templates are Common.

## First-session sequence and copy

1. The full-screen v3 splash keeps its separate logo and true loading bar. **Board ship** opens captain choice. The current art is a QA candidate; final acceptance needs Garrett's image review.
2. Four large portrait cards show species and role. The selected card can be named with a supplied default; the text field accepts 1–24 visible graphemes, rejects control characters, and persists through reload, level/rank changes, pulls, and migration. **Take command** commits the selected captain once. A retry or reload cannot add another captain.
3. The camera starts close to the bridge and adjacent rooms. A short, dismissible cue says **Drag to look around. Pinch to zoom. Tap your captain.** Selection highlights the captain and their actual location; a one-time tap opens a compact card. Gesture practice is not a blocking mini-game. Accessible Focus/zoom controls remain, but sit behind a small camera button rather than competing with the first action.
4. The hire card presents the curated Common recruit. **Hire for free** commits exactly once, shows the recruit enter through the Airlock and follow the drawn hall/doors, then highlights their appropriate work station. The player taps that station or its accessible list entry and assigns the recruit. The crew walks to its authored work anchor before the next threat cue; gameplay does not depend on decorative movement completing.
5. A trader icon and pirate ship appear in the playfield. Mandatory copy is concrete: **Pirates are firing on a trader. Help them.** One **Intercept** action starts the rescue job. There is no Contract Board or route-choice sheet in this guided beat.
6. The battle shows the Sparrow and a top-down pirate ship at matching perspective, both hull/shield states, a highlighted enemy weapons station, and the crew moving/firing at their posts. The sole taught captain order is **Target their weapons**. It cancels the pirate's next weapons volley; the crew then finish a short, deterministic, forgiving win. The order is meaningful within the same combat transition model used by the next normal pirate fight, not a purely scripted laser animation. No extra **Brace** button appears in the guided fight; Brace is introduced in a later job with its cost and effect shown then.
7. Cargo, credits, and reputation visibly reach the ship/HUD. A concise result opens the third berth. The ship name can be edited here or the supplied **Sparrow** kept. A free welcome recruit reveal names the actual drawn member, shows the correct rarity/star/role, and gives one useful next-context cue: Weapons, Shields, or future Away team. No shop card or premium currency appears.
8. Optional Jest registration follows the reward. In mock Pages QA it is clearly unavailable; in a supported Jest context it may offer sign-in. Neither screen claims that sign-in alone provides cloud recovery while persistence is browser-local. **Continue to ship** is always available.
9. The tutorial ends on the close ship view with one next-job cue. Deeper crew stats, upgrades, expedition planning, additional orders, reputation odds, and offers surface later through play. No second mandatory sequence of explanatory pop-ups starts immediately.

Mandatory copy remains at least 16 CSS px; one-line instructional cues target 18 CSS px; controls have 44×44 px minimum hit areas. The UI uses the approved worn-industrial, high-pixel-count new-retro direction: strong dark plates over art, amber for the single primary action, cyan for ship systems, red plus shape/motion for threat. Short nouns and verbs replace labels such as “push the signal” and “secure the contracts.” No text-only advancement is counted as a gameplay step.

## Combat and presentation contract

The saved crew-run encounter remains the authority for damage, orders, and payouts. Extend its pure transition with an enemy-system target and a target-weapons order. This order costs no fuel or currency, is available once per encounter at the telegraphed weapons-charge window, and cancels the next pirate weapons volley. The UI renders transition events (lock, shot, shield hit, system disable, repair, win); it does not invent results. The tutorial's authored enemy and beat sequence guarantee a quick win, while the next Reliable/Push pirate fight uses the same system without a guaranteed outcome. Existing other encounters stay on their legacy resolver until separately migrated and are not presented as equivalent combat depth.

The battle camera starts with both ships readable on a 360px phone; the Sparrow is still large enough to see its staffed rooms. Pinch/pan may move between room inspection and the two-ship overview. A clear Focus action returns to the threatened system or battle overview without overriding an active gesture. Enemy art must share the player's top-down viewpoint; the current corvette image in the mockup is composition-only and cannot ship as the pirate identity. System targets need distinct hit regions and a non-color cue. Reduced motion keeps attack timing, system-state changes, and order outcomes understandable without projectile travel.

Portrait cards, station sheets, battle HUD, and recruit reveal use one component vocabulary rather than separate web-like panels. The four captain portraits must resemble the splash cast at card size; ship sprites must remain recognizably the same identities. Art work begins with references/crops and a one-output-per-asset brief; preserve originals and record prompt, generated file, model route, hash, and disposition. Built-in Codex generation is the default for hero/portrait art; its exact backend model is not exposed, so do not claim verified “GPT Image 2.5.” Flora requires a fresh count, price estimate, cap, and separate approval before any paid run or retry. No speculative bulk asset generation is in this package.

## State, compatibility, and failure behavior

- New players receive script 5, one starter captain, no pre-hired Bolt, and an unclaimed free-hire entitlement. The captain selection, free hire, station assignment, fight start/order/win, claim, ship naming, welcome pull, and registration decision each commit an idempotent event before presentation. A repeated click or reload cannot duplicate crew, fuel cost, payout, or pull.
- Existing script-4 saves finish script 4 with their Rex/Bolt roster and current rewards. Completed, veteran, and active-contract saves never restart onboarding, lose crew, or acquire the new free hire. Migration may add nullable captain fields but cannot reinterpret a prior `captainName` as a new selected crew instance.
- A crew instance stores `customName` and `isCaptain` separately from catalog identity. Recalculation preserves those fields; all roster, station, encounter, and reward UI display the custom name where appropriate. Template identity remains stable for duplicate/star and authored abilities.
- If the first hire or guided encounter cannot validate, fail closed with a recoverable tutorial state and a clear retry action; do not fall through to the legacy resolver or grant a reward. Corrupt snapshots cannot create a second captain or free pull.
- Ship- and crew-derived combat values come from the existing station-output selector. Captain role choice may vary how a fight looks and which station is highlighted, but the guided success contract and rewards are the same for all four starts. This is tested, not just assumed.
